# Paylez Pricing Strategy — v2
### Market-anchored tiers for Poland & Uzbekistan, the Pass bundle, and the gift-card economics

**Date:** 23 September 2026 · **Markets:** Kraków (PL) · Tashkent (UZB)
**What changed in v2:** prices are now set against **researched market comparables** rather than estimates; the bundle **earns revenue** instead of giving Pro away; and there is a full treatment of the **gift-card subsidy** — the cost that consumer revenue actually exists to fund. §9 answers the points-economy question.

Three decisions carry this strategy:

**1. Price against savings apps, not entertainment apps.** Poland's most-held consumer subscription, Allegro Smart!, is **10.99 zł/month (59.90 zł/year)**. Glovo Prime is **12.99 zł/month**. That is the shelf Paylez sits on — not Spotify's 23.99 zł. Pro at **12.99 zł** matches Glovo Prime exactly, which is the right frame: *costs what Glovo Prime costs, saves you more.* The earlier 19.99 zł was roughly 60% above the real market.

**2. Charge for Pro inside the bundle — don't give it away.** At **6.99 zł/month** attached to a Pass, a single café's 182-subscriber pass base is worth **~1,240 zł/month** to Paylez (891 zł of bundled Pro at 70% attach, plus their 349 zł Scale plan). Giving Pro away free earns only the 349 zł plan fee. **The paid bundle is 3.6× better** and the user still saves 6 zł a month. Your instinct here was right.

**3. Gift cards must be Pro-only, repriced, and pool-capped — or they bankrupt the tier.** At today's rate of 50 points per złoty, a Premium user earning 4,250 points a month could claim **85 zł of Paylez-funded gift cards on a ~20 zł fee**. That is unbounded downside. Three fixes in §4 close it and turn gift cards into the reason to pay.

## 1. What the market actually charges

Prices below are researched, not estimated. They define the shelf.

### 1.1 Poland — the relevant comparables are *savings* subscriptions

| Service | Price | Category |
|---|---|---|
| **Allegro Smart!** | **10.99 zł/mo · 59.90 zł/yr** | Savings — the mass-market benchmark |
| **Glovo Prime** | **12.99 zł/mo** | Savings — free delivery + partner offers |
| Spotify Premium | ~23.99 zł/mo | Entertainment |
| Netflix | 33–60 zł/mo | Entertainment |

The lesson is the gap between the two groups. Subscriptions that **save you money** live at **11–13 zł**; subscriptions that **entertain you** live at 24–60 zł. Paylez is unambiguously the first kind, and the audience — value-seeking new arrivals — is the most price-sensitive segment of it. Allegro Smart's annual at 59.90 zł (an effective ~45% discount) also shows how strongly this market responds to deep annual pricing.

### 1.2 Uzbekistan — the relevant comparables are payment-app subscriptions

Uzbekistan has a mature, crowded market for exactly this kind of product: the payment apps all sell cashback/benefit subscriptions, and they define the price ladder.

| Service | Price | What it buys |

| IVI | ~13,000 UZS | Streaming |

| ITV Lite · Kinoman | 19,000–20,000 UZS | Streaming |

**Pro at 14,900 sits just under the Yandex Plus anchor** — deliberately, so it reads as "cheaper than the subscription everyone already has." **Premium at 29,900 lands exactly on the TBC Plus / Megogo shelf**, a well-established price point for "serious value tier." Charging 39,990 like Payme All-inclusive would be indefensible — that price buys a bank's whole transfer package.

Two tactics worth copying from this market: **Payme's intro pricing** (first 30 days at 9,990 instead of 39,990) and **Uzum's spend-to-unlock** (free if you spend 500k+/month). Both are proven here.

## 3. Why anyone pays: the two things Pro actually buys

The honest test of a savings subscription is whether it returns more than it costs. Pro's points uplift alone is worth about **7.80 zł/month** of realised discount (≈650 extra points at the ~1.2 grosz a point realises through the 10% voucher tier). Against the old 19.99 zł that was a 12 zł hole. **Against 12.99 zł it is a 5 zł hole** — and the two paid-tier exclusives close it comfortably:

**Gift cards (Paylez-funded, scarce).** The strongest reason to pay, because Free users cannot access them at all. This is the change in §4: points → vouchers for everyone, points → **gift cards for Pro and Premium only**. It creates a hard, legible paywall and — critically — confines Paylez's cash subsidy to users who are paying for it.

**Exclusive venue deals (partner-funded, free to Paylez).** The margin engine. A member-only deal costs Paylez nothing, costs the partner a marginal discount they choose, and can easily carry 10–30 zł of monthly value. This is why the exclusive-deal supply floor matters: commit to **at least 8 live member-only deals per city per month, each worth 10 zł+**. If that supply isn't there, Pro isn't worth 12.99 zł and the churn will say so in month two.

Everything else — the multiplier, energy, voucher validity, streak freezes, assistant — is engagement value at near-zero marginal cost. Useful for retention, not load-bearing for the price.

## 4. Gift cards: the subsidy that consumer revenue exists to fund

This is the part that decides whether the consumer tier makes or loses money, and it needs fixing before launch.

### 4.1 The problem, quantified

Today gift cards price at **50 points per złoty** (500 pts → 10 zł, 2,500 pts → 50 zł). Vouchers realise roughly **1.2 grosz per point**; gift cards pay out at **2 grosz per point**. So the reward Paylez funds in cash is *more generous* than the one partners fund — precisely backwards.

| Tier | Points earned/month | Gift-card value if fully redeemed |
|---|---|---|
| Free | 2,300 | **46 zł** |
| Pro | 2,950 | **59 zł** |
| Premium | 4,250 | **85 zł** |

A Premium user could claim 85 zł of Paylez-funded cards against a 19.99 zł fee, and a Free user 46 zł against nothing at all. Unbounded, and worst on the users who pay least.

### 4.2 The fix — three levers, use all three

**1. Gift cards become Pro and Premium only.** Free users spend points on partner-funded vouchers, which cost Paylez nothing. This aligns the cost with the revenue: *the people who can spend Paylez's money are the people paying Paylez.* It is also the cleanest upgrade hook in the product.

**2. Reprice gift cards to 100 points per złoty.** A 50 zł card costs 5,000 points — roughly six weeks of Premium earning. That halves exposure per point and, correctly, makes the Paylez-funded reward *less* generous than the partner-funded one.

**3. Fund them from a capped monthly pool — 20% of the prior month's consumer subscription revenue.** This is the lever that actually bounds the risk, because the pool, not the earn rate, becomes the binding constraint:

| Paying users | Consumer revenue | Gift-card pool (20%) | 50 zł cards available |
|---|---|---|---|
| 200 | 2,800 zł | 560 zł | ~11 / month |
| 1,000 | 14,000 zł | 2,800 zł | ~56 / month |
| 5,000 | 70,000 zł | 14,000 zł | ~280 / month |

The scarcity this creates — *"5 left this month" · "All gone. New cards on the 1st"* — is already in the app's design, and it is the correct mechanism. It converts an uncapped liability into a fixed cost line **and** makes the reward feel valuable. Keep the stock counters prominent; they are doing real financial work.

### 4.3 What a paying user is worth after the subsidy

| | Price | Store fee (~10%) | Payment (~2.5%) | Gift pool (20%) | Infra | **Contribution** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pro** | 12.99 zł | 1.30 | 0.32 | 2.60 | 0.80 | **7.97 zł (61%)** |
| **Premium** | 19.99 zł | 2.00 | 0.50 | 4.00 | 0.80 | **12.69 zł (63%)** |
| **Bundled Pro** | 6.99 zł | 0.70 | 0.17 | 1.40 | 0.80 | **3.92 zł (56%)** |

Healthy at every tier. One note on the store fee: Apple and Google take **15–30%** on in-app purchases, which at these price points is the single largest cost line after the subsidy. Under the EU's Digital Markets Act you can steer users to **web checkout** in Poland — worth building, as it recovers most of that 10–15%.

## 6. The bundle — and why it should cost money

### 6.1 The offer

At Pass checkout, Pro is offered at **6.99 zł/month** (46% off) — pre-ticked, one tap, cancellable separately.

| | Bought separately | **Bundled** |
|---|---|---|
| Daily Brew Pass | 49 zł | 49 zł |
| Paylez Pro | 12.99 zł | **6.99 zł** |
| **User pays** | **61.99 zł** | **55.99 zł** *(saves 6 zł)* |
| Partner receives | 49 zł (100%) | **49 zł (100%)** |
| **Paylez receives** | 12.99 zł | **6.99 zł** |

### 6.2 Why paid beats free

Taking the demo café's 182 pass subscribers across two live passes:

| Model | Paylez monthly revenue from that venue |
|---|---|
| Pro given away free | **349 zł** (Scale plan only) |
| **Pro bundled at 6.99 zł, 70% attach** | **1,240 zł** (891 zł bundled Pro + 349 zł plan) |
| Pro bundled at 6.99 zł, 90% attach | 1,494 zł |

**3.6× better at a realistic attach rate**, and the user still gets a visible saving. Free Pro would have been leaving the single best-converting audience in the product — people already paying a venue every month — entirely unmonetised. And unlike cold conversion, this audience converts: they are at checkout, already spending, and the add-on is 6.99 zł.

### 6.3 Rules

- **Premium for Pass-holders: 14.99 zł** (25% off) — the tier keeps its job and its upsell path.
- **Growth and Scale partners only** — Starter can't sell Passes, so it never applies.
- **One subscription per user**, however many Passes they hold. Three Passes don't mean three Pros.
- **Already paying full price?** Drop them to the bundled rate automatically. Never charge a loyal subscriber more than a new one for the same thing.
- **Pass ends → bundled rate ends**, with a grace window and a one-tap offer to stay at the standard price. A well-timed, well-earned conversion moment.

## 7. What changes in Uzbekistan

**Lead with prepaid, not auto-renew.** Payme, Click and Uzum all support card-on-file, but auto-renewal is a weaker norm and involuntary churn from card expiry runs higher. Present **3-month and annual prepaid** as the primary options, with monthly auto-renew as the alternative — the inverse of the Polish presentation. The market has trained users this way; Payme and Click both sell prepaid periods.

**Use an intro price.** Payme sells its 39,990 tier at 9,990 for the first 30 days. Mirror it: **Pro at 4,900 UZS for the first month.** It is a proven local pattern and removes all first-purchase friction.

**Expect a different revenue mix.** Consumer/partner will run closer to **25/75 in Tashkent** versus roughly 50/50 in Kraków at launch. Free has to be genuinely complete there — it is the product for most Uzbek users.

**Gift cards need local stock.** The subsidy pool logic is identical, but the cards themselves must be locally meaningful (Uzum Market, Korzinka, local fuel/telecom) — an Empik card is worthless in Tashkent. Pool at the same 20% of local consumer revenue.

**Verify FX at launch.** Figures assume roughly 3,400 UZS to the złoty. Money is stored in the venue's currency in minor units; display conversion only.

## 8. Pricing guidance for partners setting their own Pass

A loss-making Pass churns a partner faster than a high price does. The rule to enforce in the builder's economics helper:

> **Minimum viable price  P ≥ (R × C) − U**
> **R** = redemptions per subscriber per month · **C** = COGS per redeemed item · **U** = incremental upsell gross profit per subscriber per month

Against the demo pass (49 zł, 128 subscribers, 2,104 redemptions, 8,940 zł upsell → R = 16.4, U ≈ 48.90 zł):

| COGS per redemption | Monthly cost | Fees + upsell profit | **Net** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3.50 zł** (filter coffee) | 7,364 zł | 12,530 zł | **+5,166 zł** |
| **5.96 zł** | 12,540 zł | 12,530 zł | **break-even** |
| **9.00 zł** | 18,936 zł | 12,530 zł | **−6,406 zł** |

Two rules follow, both already half-built into the Pass builder:

- **Cap redemptions by default.** Unlimited is what forced Panera to retrofit a 30-drink monthly cap *and* raise the price. Keep the unlimited warning non-skippable.
- **The pass must drive upsell or the price must rise.** Remove the upsell from that table and the same pass needs **58 zł**, not 49. Pret's Club Pret works because subscribers visit ~5× more and buy food on most visits — the fee fills seats, the food is the profit.

**Opening bands:**

| Pass type | Poland | Uzbekistan |
|---|---|---|
| Daily allowance (1 item/day) | 45–69 zł | 129,000–169,000 UZS |
| Monthly bundle (10 items) | 69–89 zł | 149,000–199,000 UZS |
| VIP perks (% off + perks) | 29–39 zł | 59,000–89,000 UZS |
| Weekend / seasonal | 25–35 zł | 49,000–79,000 UZS |

## 9. Can everything run on points? — the closed-loop economy question

Short answer: **technically yes, commercially tempting, and legally the most dangerous thing in this document.** Build the 80% that is safe; do not build the part that sells points for cash.

### 9.1 What breaks if points become purchasable

**It puts Paylez inside the money flow.** The entire Passes architecture works because money moves **customer → partner directly** (Stripe in PL; Payme/Click/Uzum in UZB) and Paylez never holds funds — which is what keeps Paylez out of payments licensing. If a user buys points with cash and spends them on a Pass, Paylez is now taking customer money and settling with merchants. That is the exact thing the architecture was designed to avoid, in both markets.

**It may constitute e-money or a payment service.** In the EU, a balance bought with cash and redeemable for goods from multiple unrelated merchants looks like **electronic money** (EMD2) or a payment instrument under **PSD2**. There is a limited-network exemption, but a city-wide, multi-category venue network with a cash-purchasable balance strains it, and exceeding the volume threshold triggers a notification duty to the regulator (KNF in Poland). Uzbekistan has its own e-money and currency-control regime that needs separate advice.

**VAT gets complicated.** Under EU rules a multi-purpose voucher is taxed at redemption, not at sale, with deferred-revenue and breakage accounting behind it. That is a finance-function problem, not a feature flag.

**And it would wreck the game loop.** If points can be bought, earning them stops meaning anything. The games are Paylez's cheapest engagement engine and its clearest differentiator for this audience; making their output purchasable devalues the whole thing. This is a well-documented failure mode in loyalty design, and it is the reason to decline even setting the legal question aside.

*Not legal advice — confirm with Polish and Uzbek counsel before going near this.*

### 9.2 The safe version — which gets you most of what you want

Keep **points earn-only**. They are never sold for cash. Then:

- **Subscriptions grant points.** Pro grants 300/month, Premium 1,000/month. This is a subscription benefit, not a currency sale — legally ordinary loyalty practice, and it gives you the "Pro comes with points" mechanic you described.
- **Points buy partner vouchers** (partner-funded, costs Paylez nothing) — the main sink, ~95% of volume.
- **Points buy gift cards** (Paylez-funded, Pro+ only, pool-capped) — the aspirational sink, per §4.
- **Points buy a *discount* on a Pass, never the Pass itself.** For example 2,000 points = 50% off your first month, funded by the partner as acquisition or from a capped Paylez promo budget. The Pass is still paid in cash, customer → partner. **This gives you "passes cost points" in the user's experience without moving a single złoty through Paylez.**

That last line is the key unlock: the *feeling* of a unified points economy, with none of the licensing, VAT, or float consequences.

### 9.3 If you still want a real wallet later

Treat it as a deliberate regulatory project, not a feature: either **partner with a licensed e-money institution** so the regulated entity holds the funds and Paylez stays an agent, or **restrict purchasable balance to a single venue** (a single-purpose voucher, materially simpler). Revisit once there is scale worth the compliance cost — not at launch.

## 11. Risks

**Pro churns in month two.** The tell is cancellation before second renewal; the cause is almost always thin exclusive-deal supply. Watch weekly in month one — the fix is partner-side, not price-side.

**The gift-card pool gets raided by a few heavy users.** Mitigate with a per-user cap (e.g. one card per 60 days) on top of the pool, so scarcity is distributed rather than captured.

**Bundle attach is low.** If under ~40%, the problem is checkout placement, not price. Fix presentation before cutting to free — free is a 3.6× revenue cut.

**Store commission erodes thin margins.** At 12.99 zł, a 30% store fee is 3.90 zł. Build web checkout for Poland under the DMA; in Uzbekistan, Payme/Click/Uzum billing avoids it entirely.

**Uzbek involuntary churn.** Track failed renewals separately from voluntary cancellations — different problem, different fix. Prepaid periods are the mitigation.

**Price changes erode trust.** Policy now: **existing subscribers keep their price until they cancel**, both sides; new prices apply to new subscribers and at renewal only. The Passes feature already works this way for partners' subscribers — hold Paylez to the same standard.
